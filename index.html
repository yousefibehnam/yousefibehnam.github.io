About

Researcher, engineer,
photographer & nature explorer

My name is Behnam Yousefi, and I am an IT engineer at the Telecommunication Company of Iran, where I have kept enterprise‑scale network services running since 2012.

Passionate about software engineering and machine learning, my research focuses on improving the quality, reliability and maintainability of software systems. Specifically, I measure object‑oriented code through static analysis, and I use machine learning to detect anomalies in service logs and attacks in network traffic.

I hold a master’s degree in Information Technology Engineering from Islamic Azad University, where I ranked first in my cohort and received the Distinguished Master’s Thesis Award for research on software quality assessment.

Outside of research, I am a photographer and a nature explorer. Camping in foggy mountains and long off‑road trips have taught me to plan for what can fail and to notice the small details, which I bring both to the lab and to the trail.

Multisource Log Fusion

Anomaly Detection for IoT Services by fusing call, business and metric logs

One log stream rarely tells the whole story. Combining three log types made it possible to detect anomalies and follow how a fault propagates between enterprise services, using statistical and graph‑based methods.

Hybrid Intrusion Detection

A PSO‑optimized SVM with engineered flow‑based features

Statistical and flow‑based traffic features feed an SVM whose parameters are tuned by particle swarm optimization, compared against genetic‑algorithm feature selection across multiple attack scenarios.

Industry project

Network Reliability at TCI

Recurring fault analysis and monitoring automation for enterprise telecom infrastructure.

2012 — present

In production, reliability is a daily practice. I collect and analyze operational logs and performance indicators from enterprise network infrastructure to find the faults that keep coming back, perform root‑cause analysis across hardware, software and network incidents, and support system deployment and service optimization.

Much of the routine is now automated: Python and shell scripts gather device status, flag anomalies and produce daily reports.

This is where my research questions come from: real systems, real logs, real failures.
